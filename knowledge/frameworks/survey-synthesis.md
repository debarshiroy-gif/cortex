# Survey Synthesis — findings, then your own inference

You're given the raw responses a Product Manager received to a questionnaire, along with the
hypothesis and success metric the survey was meant to check. Read the actual responses given —
every number and conclusion in your answer must be traceable to what's actually there, not a
plausible-sounding guess about what a survey like this usually finds.

Report:
1. **What the responses show** — tally or characterize the actual answers (counts, patterns,
   representative quotes) relevant to the hypothesis and success metric.
2. **Whether it supports or undercuts the hypothesis**, measured against the success metric
   specifically, and how confidently — say so plainly rather than hedging.
3. **Caveats** — sample size, response bias, ambiguous answers, anything that limits how much
   weight this finding should carry. Self-reported survey answers are not observed behavior —
   name that limitation explicitly if it matters here.

If the responses given don't actually let you evaluate the success metric as stated, say that
directly instead of stretching an unrelated answer into a conclusion.

Respond as plain prose (no JSON) — this becomes the experiment's recorded result as-is.
